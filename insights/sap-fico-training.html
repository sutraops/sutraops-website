1 SAP & Enterprise Structure

The organizational units that define how data, documents, and reports are structured.

Meaning

What is it?

Enterprise structure defines the legal, organizational, and management boundaries within which business transactions are recorded. Every document you post carries a piece of this structure — Company Code, Plant, Cost Center, Profit Center.

Key Organizational Units

Client (Mandant)
Top-level container. Self-contained commercial unit with its own master data.
Company Code (BUKRS)
Independent legal entity. Balance sheet and P&L produced here.
Controlling Area (KOKRS)
Management accounting boundary. Multiple company codes can share one.
Business Area (GSBER)
Cross-company-code segment for internal reporting.
Functional Area (FKBER)
Classifies expenses by function — production, sales, admin, R&D.
Plant (WERKS)
Operating facility within a company code. Central to MM, PP, ML.
Cost Center (KOSTL)
Where overhead costs are incurred. Departmental reporting.
Profit Center (PRCTR)
Internal profitability unit. Produces internal P&L and balance sheet.
Segment (SEGMENT)
Reporting segment for IFRS 8 / Ind AS 108.
House Bank (BANKL)
Bank relationship for a company code — used in Bank Accounting (BL).
IHC Company Code
Dedicated company code for In-House Cash Centre.
Valuation Area (BWKEY)
Organizational unit for material valuation — usually a plant. Central to ML.

Real-World Example

Scenario — ABC Group

Company: ABC Manufacturing Group, with operations in India, Singapore, Germany.

  • Client 100
  • Company Codes: IN01 (India), SG01 (Singapore), DE01 (Germany)
  • Controlling Area ABCG — shared across IN01, SG01, DE01
  • Plants: PL01 (Pune), PL02 (Chennai), PL03 (Singapore)
  • Cost Centers: CC-PROD, CC-QA, CC-MKT, CC-FIN, CC-IT
  • Profit Centers: PC-IND, PC-CON, PC-EXPORT
  • House Banks: HDFC, ICICI (India); DBS (Singapore)

Interview Prep

Q1: Can one controlling area be assigned to multiple company codes?
Yes — provided they share the same chart of accounts, fiscal year variant, and currency (or have currency translation configured). Common in companies with multiple legal entities managed centrally.
Q2: Business Area vs Profit Center?
Business Area (GSBER) is a legacy cross-company-code unit. Profit Center (PRCTR) is the modern equivalent — it produces a full internal P&L and balance sheet and is used for segment reporting.

2 General Ledger & Core FI

The heart of financial accounting — how every transaction is recorded, classified, and reported.

Meaning

What is it?

The General Ledger (GL) is the central repository of all financial transactions. Every posting in AP, AR, AA, BL, IHB, or CO flows through the GL. GL accounts are defined at two levels: Chart of Accounts (SKA1) and Company Code (SKB1).

Core Concepts

GL Account Master
SKA1 + SKB1. Determines P&L or balance sheet classification, currency, field status.
Document Type
Two-char code (SA, KR, DR) controlling number range, posting keys, reversal.
Posting Key
40=debit GL, 50=credit GL, 31=credit vendor, 21=debit vendor, 01=debit customer.
Number Range
Determines document number sequence per document type and company code.
Field Status Group
Controls required/optional/suppressed fields per GL account.
Posting Period
Open periods — normal (1–12) and special (13–16) for adjustments.
Parallel Ledgers
Leading ledger (0L) + non-leading (IFRS, tax, local GAAP).
Parked Documents
Saved but not posted. Do not affect balances. Used for approval workflows.
Substitutions & Validations
Rules that auto-fill or validate fields at posting time (OBBH / OB28).
Document Splitting
New GL feature — every document balanced by profit center / segment.

Example — Journal Entry

Scenario

ABC Ltd. pays office rent of ₹50,000 for April 2024 by bank transfer.

Interview Prep

Q1: Posting key vs document type?
Document type classifies the entire transaction and controls number range and allowed account types. Posting key applies to a single line and determines debit/credit and account type.
Q2: How does SAP ensure debits equal credits?
SAP enforces the double-entry rule at save. If totals differ, the document is rejected with "Document is not balanced."
Q3: What is document splitting?
A New GL feature ensuring documents are balanced not only in total but also by dimensions like profit center or segment — enabling real-time segment reporting.

3 Accounts Payable, Receivable & Asset Accounting

The three subledgers that sit beside the GL — each with its own master data, transactions, and reconciliation.

Meaning

What is it?

AP, AR, and AA are subledgers — they maintain detailed records for specific account types and post summarised balances to the GL via reconciliation accounts. At month end, subledger balance must equal GL reconciliation account balance.

Accounts Payable (AP)

Master Data: LFA1 (general), LFB1 (company code), LFM1 (purchasing).

Interview Prep

Q1: What is a reconciliation account?
The GL account linked to a subledger (AP, AR, AA). You cannot post directly to it — all postings go through the subledger. Guarantees subledger = GL at any point.
Q2: What is GR/IR clearing?
A suspense account. GR debits inventory, credits GR/IR. Invoice debits GR/IR, credits vendor. If matched, zero balance. Residual is reclassified with F.19 at month end.
Q3: How is depreciation calculated?
By asset class → depreciation key → method, life, rate → depreciation area. Posted via AFAB. Entry: Dr Expense / Cr Accumulated Depreciation.

4 Bank Accounting (BL)

Managing cash, bank statements, reconciliations, and cash flow — the link between the bank and the GL.

Meaning

What is it?

Bank Accounting (BL) manages the company's relationships with banks: recording payments, receiving bank statements, matching them to GL entries, and forecasting cash. It answers: "How much cash do we have? What's coming in? What's going out? Does the bank balance match our books?"

Bank Accounting consists of three connected sub-areas: Bank Master Data, Electronic Bank Statement (EBS), and Cash Management / Liquidity Forecast.

Core Concepts

House Bank
A bank the company has a relationship with (HDFC, ICICI, DBS). Defined in FI12 for each company code.
Bank Account ID
Specific account within a house bank (current account, OD account, etc.). The pair (house bank + account ID) = your bank account.
Bank Directory
Master list of banks, bank keys, and routing numbers. Maintained via BA01 / FI01.
Bank Statement
File received from bank (MT940 / MT942 / CAMT.053 / BAI2) listing all transactions in the account.
EBS — Electronic Bank Statement
Automated processing of bank statement files. Transactions are matched to open items automatically.
Lockbox
Bank-operated PO box for customer payments. Bank sends a lockbox file; SAP matches to customer invoices.
Manual Bank Statement
Entered manually via FF67 for banks that don't send electronic files.
Cash Position
Snapshot of cash at a point in time (end of day) — FF7A.
Liquidity Forecast
Forward projection of cash inflows/outflows — FF7B.
Bank Reconciliation
Monthly comparison of bank balance vs GL balance. Difference = unreconciled items.
Payment Medium Workbench
Generates payment files (NACH, ACH, SWIFT) in bank-specific formats — DMEE.
Bank Chain
Sequence of banks involved in an international payment (intermediary banks).

Cash Management & Liquidity Forecast

Cash Management (FF7A): Shows actual cash and bank balances at end of day, by bank account and currency. Provides a snapshot of liquidity.

Liquidity Forecast (FF7B): Forward-looking projection combining:

  • Open customer invoices (expected inflows)
  • Open vendor invoices (expected outflows)
  • Planned payments and receipts
  • One-time and periodic postings

Both are configured via Cash Management groups (OT51) and planning levels.

Common Errors & Fixes

"Account symbol not assigned to bank account"
EBS cannot determine the GL account for a transaction type.
Review OTB1 / OT01 configuration.
"No posting rule found"
Bank account has no posting rule assigned.
Assign posting rule via OTB5 / OT06.
"Bank statement file format invalid"
MT940 variant mismatch.
Check OT83 format definition matches bank's actual file.
"Manual bank statement items pending"
Auto-matching failed for some items.
Process in FEBAN — assign manually or adjust algorithms.
"Bank balance ≠ GL balance"
Unreconciled items (outstanding cheques, deposits in transit).
Run bank reconciliation report; investigate stale items with the bank.
"Cash position does not match"
Bank sub-accounts not mapped to cash management groups.
Maintain mapping via OT51 / transaction FF7A config.

Interview Prep

Q1: What is the difference between House Bank and Account ID?
A House Bank is the banking partner (e.g., HDFC Bank). An Account ID identifies a specific account within that bank (current account, OD account, ESCROW). Together they form the unique bank identifier in SAP: BANKL + BNKACC. You can have multiple account IDs under one house bank.
Q2: How does EBS auto-match a customer payment?
EBS uses a three-layer approach: (1) Interpretation algorithms identify the transaction type from the bank code and text fields. (2) Posting rules define which algorithms run and in what order. (3) Search algorithms look up the matching open item — by invoice number, amount, or customer code. If a match is found, the item is auto-cleared. If not, it goes to FEBAN for manual processing.
Q3: What is the difference between Cash Position and Liquidity Forecast?
Cash Position (FF7A) is a snapshot of current bank balances — what you have now. Liquidity Forecast (FF7B) is a projection into the future — what you expect to receive and pay, based on open items and planned transactions. Cash Position looks backward and at today; Liquidity Forecast looks forward.
Q4: What is lockbox and how is it different from EBS?
A lockbox is a physical PO box operated by the bank for customer payments. The bank collects the payments, processes them, and sends SAP a lockbox file (BAI2 format). SAP matches the payments to customer invoices using invoice numbers or MICR data. EBS is a broader concept — it processes the entire bank statement (all transactions). Lockbox is a specific tool for automating customer receipt matching at high volume.
Q5: How do you reconcile the bank statement with the GL?
After EBS is fully processed, the GL bank account balance should equal the bank statement's closing balance. Any difference is due to: outstanding cheques, deposits in transit, bank errors, or unrecorded bank charges. Use the bank reconciliation report, review unreconciled items, and clear them once the underlying transaction is recorded.

5 In-House Banking / In-House Cash (IHB / IHC)

How large groups centralise cash, net intercompany payments, and manage internal liquidity — using an internal bank structure.

Meaning

What is it?

In-House Banking (also called In-House Cash / IHC) is a treasury tool that lets a group act as its own bank. Instead of every subsidiary maintaining multiple external bank accounts and making cross-border payments to each other, the group sets up an In-House Cash Centre that acts as an internal bank. All intercompany payments flow through the IHC. The IHC then consolidates and nets external payments, reducing bank fees, FX exposure, and cash trapped in subsidiaries.

IHC is a Treasury (TRM) submodule, but it posts directly to FI — so FICO consultants frequently work with it.

Why Companies Use IHC

  • Cash visibility: Treasury has real-time visibility of all subsidiary cash positions.
  • Netting: Intercompany payables and receivables offset each other — only net payments are made.
  • Reduced bank fees: Fewer external transactions = lower transaction fees.
  • FX consolidation: FX is managed centrally, not in each subsidiary.
  • Internal lending: Group can lend to subsidiaries at internal rates.
  • Better cash pooling: Physical or notional pooling of balances.

Core Concepts

In-House Cash Centre (IHCC)
A dedicated company code representing the internal bank. Acts as the counterparty for all intercompany transactions.
IHC Account
An internal account held by each subsidiary with the IHC. Equivalent to a bank account, but internal to the group.
Internal Customer
The subsidiary as a customer of the IHC. Created in IHC customer master.
Internal Vendor
The IHC as a vendor to the subsidiary. Created as an IHC vendor.
Payment Order
An instruction to the IHC to pay on behalf of a subsidiary. Central to IHC processing.
Netting
Offsetting payables and receivables between subsidiaries. Only the net is settled.
Physical Cash Pooling
Actual transfer of funds to a concentration account.
Notional Pooling
Balances aggregated for interest calculation only — no physical transfer.
Internal Loan
Loan from IHC to a subsidiary, with internal interest calculation.
IHC FX
Currency conversion handled by IHC on behalf of subsidiaries.

Real-World Example — Intercompany Netting

Scenario — ABC Group

Three subsidiaries trade with each other:

  • ABC India (IN01) owes ABC Singapore (SG01) ₹50,00,000
  • ABC Singapore (SG01) owes ABC Germany (DE01) €50,000 (₹45,00,000)
  • ABC Germany (DE01) owes ABC India (IN01) €55,000 (₹49,50,000)

Without IHC, ABC Group would make 3 external cross-border payments.

With IHC: All three subsidiaries post their intercompany invoices to the IHC as if the IHC were their bank. The IHC computes net positions and settles only the net amounts.

SG0145,00,00050,00,000+5,00,000 (receive)

Only 2 net payments are made instead of 3 gross payments — and all through the IHC rather than between subsidiaries.

Interview Prep

Q1: What is the purpose of In-House Cash?
IHC centralises cash management across a group. It acts as an internal bank — subsidiaries hold internal accounts with the IHC instead of external bank accounts for intercompany flows. This enables netting, centralised FX, internal lending, and dramatic reduction in external bank transactions and fees.
Q2: How does netting work in IHC?
Subsidiaries post their intercompany payables and receivables to the IHC instead of directly to each other. At defined cut-off dates (daily, weekly, monthly), the IHC computes net positions per subsidiary. Only the net is settled externally. Example: if IN01 owes SG01 ₹50L and SG01 owes IN01 ₹49.5L, only ₹0.5L is settled instead of ₹99.5L.
Q3: How is IHC different from regular bank accounting?
Bank Accounting deals with external banks — statements come from the bank (MT940), payments go to external beneficiaries. IHC deals with internal group transactions — statements are generated internally, payments flow between subsidiaries and the IHC. IHC is a Treasury module, not pure FI, but it produces FI postings.
Q4: What are the prerequisites for implementing IHC?
(1) A dedicated company code for the IHC. (2) Customer and vendor master records for the IHC. (3) Internal accounts created for each participating subsidiary. (4) Clear intercompany reconciliation accounts. (5) Defined payment order types and posting rules. (6) Netting configuration if netting is used. (7) Agreement on internal FX rates and interest calculation.

6 Controlling (CO) — The Full Landscape

Complete CO coverage: Cost Center Accounting (CO-CC), Cost Element Accounting (CO-CEL), Internal Orders (CO-OPA), Activity-Based Costing (CO-ABC), Product Costing (CO-PC), Cost Object Controlling / Production (CO-PP), Profitability Analysis (CO-PA), and Profit Center Accounting (PCA).

Meaning

What is CO?

Controlling (CO) is the management accounting module. It captures costs and revenues by cost object — cost centers, internal orders, production orders, profit centers, profitability segments — and answers: Where did the money go? Which department overspent? Which product is most profitable? Where can we cut costs?

CO is where the deepest financial insight lives.

Interview Prep

Q1: Primary vs secondary cost element?
Primary has an FI GL counterpart (salary expense, rent). Secondary exists only in CO and represents internal flows (assessment, distribution, activity allocation).
Q2: Distribution vs assessment?
Distribution preserves original cost elements (receiver sees the underlying cost). Assessment consolidates into one secondary cost element.
Q3: Cost center vs profit center?
Cost center collects costs by department. Profit center produces an internal P&L by product line, region, or business — it receives both costs and revenues.
Q4: What is the difference between CO-PA and PCA?
CO-PA analyzes margin by market segment (customer, product, region) — external-facing view. PCA analyzes profit by internal organizational unit (division, region) — internal view. Both reconcile to FI.
Q5: How does CO-PP tie to CO-PC?
CO-PC calculates the standard cost estimate before production. CO-PP collects actual costs during production and compares them to the standard to calculate variance. The standard cost from CO-PC is the benchmark used by CO-PP.

7 Product Costing (CO-PC) — Detailed Walkthrough

How SAP calculates the standard cost of a product and tracks actual costs on production orders.

Meaning

What is it?

Product Costing answers: "How much does it cost to make this product?" It uses the Bill of Materials (BOM) and Routing to estimate costs before production, then tracks actual costs during and after production to calculate variances. It is the bridge between FI, CO, MM, PP, and Material Ledger.

Core Concepts

BOM
List of raw materials and components required for one unit.
Routing
Operations (work centers) with standard times.
Cost Estimate
Calculated cost from BOM, routing, activity prices, overhead rates.
Costing Variant
Controls calculation logic — valuation, overhead, cost component split.
Cost Component Split
Breakdown by material, labour, overhead, etc.
Standard Cost
Expected cost, marked and released for the fiscal year. Used for inventory valuation.
Production Order
Cost object collecting actual manufacturing costs.
WIP
Value of partially completed production at period end.
Variance
Actual vs standard — analysed by input price, quantity, resource usage.
Settlement
Transfer variance to COGS, inventory, or CO-PA.

Interview Prep

Q1: Standard cost vs actual cost?
Standard = pre-calculated expected cost (used for inventory valuation). Actual = real cost incurred. Difference = variance.
Q2: What is WIP and when is it calculated?
Work in Process = value of partially completed production at period end. Calculated via KKAO. Prevents P&L distortion by deferring costs related to unfinished work.

8 Material Ledger (ML)

Actual costing, price differences, and multi-currency valuation — the bridge between MM, PP, and FI/CO.

Meaning

What is it?

The Material Ledger (ML) records the actual cost of materials, in multiple currencies and multiple valuation approaches. It tracks every goods movement for a material, compares standard cost to actual cost, and calculates price differences that are posted to inventory or COGS at period end.

In S/4HANA, Material Ledger is mandatory and is the foundation of actual costing.

Why Material Ledger Matters

  • Actual costing: Not just standard — real cost of materials in real currency.
  • Multi-currency: Value materials in company code currency, group currency, and hard currency.
  • Price differences: Purchase price variance (PPV) is tracked and settled at period end.
  • Valuation alternatives: Legal valuation, group valuation, profit center valuation.
  • Foundation for CO-PA: Actual cost component split feeds profitability analysis.
  • Inventory accuracy: Ensures inventory is valued at true cost, not just standard.

Core Concepts

Valuation Area (BWKEY)
Usually the plant. Each material-plant combination has its own valuation record.
Standard Price (S)
Fixed price. Price differences are collected and settled at period end.
Moving Average Price (V)
Recalculated with every goods movement. Price differences absorbed immediately.
Price Difference Categories
PPV (purchase), PRD (production), PRV (other), KDM/KDV (exchange rate).
Cost Component Split
Breakdown of cost into material, labour, overhead — used for CO-PA.
Periodic Actual Costing
Month-end run (CKMLCP) that revalues inventory and COGS to actual cost.
Single-Level vs Multilevel
Single-level revalues each material independently. Multilevel propagates differences through the BOM.
Valuation Views
Legal view (statutory), Group view (consolidated), Profit Center view (management).
Material Ledger Document
Every movement creates an ML document — records quantity, value, price, currency.
Settlement
At period end, price differences are settled to inventory or COGS.

The Actual Costing Run (CKMLCP)

The monthly actual costing run is the heart of ML. Sequence:

  1. Selection: Choose materials, period, valuation area.
  2. Determination: Collect all goods movements, invoices, and production postings.
  3. Multilevel Price Determination: Propagate price differences through the BOM.
  4. Revaluation: Revalue inventory to actual cost.
  5. WIP: Calculate WIP for production orders (interfaces with CO-PP).
  6. Settlement: Post differences to inventory and COGS.
  7. Post-Close: Lock the period, mark as closed.

T-codes: CKMLCP Actual Costing Run CKM3 Material Price Analysis CKMLCPAVR Alternative Valuation Run CKMLQS Quantity Structure

Material Ledger in S/4HANA

Key change

In S/4HANA, Material Ledger is mandatory. It is no longer an optional add-on. All materials, in all valuation areas, use ML. The ML data is integrated into ACDOCA — every ML posting also appears in the Universal Journal. This eliminates the historical reconciliation between ML and FI.

"Actual costing run already executed"

Reverse the prior run or use post-close step cautiously.
"Price difference not distributed"

Expand selection criteria or check material status.
"Multilevel price determination fails"

"Currency type not defined"

"Inventory differs from ML value"

Interview Prep

Q1: What is the purpose of Material Ledger?
ML records actual cost of materials in multiple currencies and valuation views, calculates price differences, and enables monthly actual costing. It bridges the gap between standard cost (used for daily postings) and actual cost (true economic cost).
Q2: How does ML handle price differences?
When a material is purchased at a price different from standard, the difference is posted to a Material Ledger price-difference account (not P&L). At month end, the actual costing run (CKMLCP) distributes this difference proportionally between inventory (remaining) and COGS (consumed).
Q3: What is single-level vs multilevel price determination?
Single-level revalues a material based on its own price differences. Multilevel propagates differences through the BOM — e.g., if raw material is ₹5 more, then every finished good that uses it inherits part of that difference. Multilevel gives a truer cost for manufactured products.
Q4: What changes in S/4HANA for Material Ledger?
ML becomes mandatory. It is integrated into ACDOCA (Universal Journal), eliminating the traditional ML-FI reconciliation. The actual costing run (CKMLCP) is still used but now writes to ACDOCA. The Material Ledger document (MLIT) replaces the older CKMLPP/CKMLCR views for most reporting.
Q5: How does Material Ledger support CO-PA?
ML produces an actual cost component split — material, labour, overhead — for every material. CO-PA uses this to build a cost-of-sales view by product. Without ML, CO-PA would only have standard cost; with ML, CO-PA reflects the true cost of goods sold, including purchase price variances.

  • Custom reports — any report reading COSP/COSS/COEP/CE1xxxx must be reviewed. In S/4HANA these are compatibility views on ACDOCA — performance may suffer.

  • Credit Management — FI-AR credit management replaced by FSCM.
  • Classic CO-PA — costing-based CO-PA continues but stored in ACDOCA. Account-based is the recommended path.

Interview Prep

Q1: What is the Universal Journal?
ACDOCA — single table for all financial line items (FI, CO, AA, ML, CO-PA). Eliminates FI-CO reconciliation. Enables real-time analytics.
Q2: How does OBYC work?
OBYC maps transaction keys (BSX, WRX, PRD, GBB) + chart of accounts + valuation class + valuation grouping code → GL account. Determines which accounts get debited/credited in MM-FI postings.
Q3: Biggest S/4HANA FI/CO challenge?
Volume of custom code referencing old tables (COSP, COSS, COEP, CE1xxxx). Migration requires rewriting reports against ACDOCA. Also ML activation and BP migration are significant workstreams.

Answer Framework

The 5-Point Framework

Scenario-Based Questions

Scenario 1: A vendor invoice of ₹1,18,000 (18% GST) is posted and paid 30 days later with 2% cash discount. Show both entries.

Invoice (FB60):

  • Dr Expense / Inventory — ₹1,00,000
  • Dr GST Input — ₹18,000
  • Cr Vendor — ₹1,18,000

Payment with discount (F-53):

  • Dr Vendor — ₹1,18,000
  • Cr Bank — ₹1,15,640
  • Cr Cash Discount Received — ₹2,000
  • Cr GST Adjustment — ₹360 (reversal of input credit on discount)

Config: Payment terms (OBB8) with 2%/10 days. Discount calculated on base amount only.

Scenario 2: A production order for 1,000 units costs ₹5,10,000 actual; standard is ₹5,00,000. How is the variance calculated and settled?

Variance (KKS1) decomposed into:

  • Input price variance (material purchase price)
  • Input quantity variance (more material used)
  • Resource usage variance (more hours)
  • Remaining variance (overhead)

Settlement (KO88): Dr Variance ₹10,000 / Cr Production Order ₹10,000. Order balance → zero.

Scenario 3: Vendor reconciliation account doesn't match AP subledger. How do you investigate?

Steps:

  1. Compare FBL1N (subledger) to FAGLL03 (GL recon account).
  2. Filter GL for manual document types (SA, DR) — direct postings to recon accounts are usually the cause.
  3. Check for parked/blocked documents, reversal errors.
  4. Reverse or correct. Recommend field status group that blocks direct posting.
Scenario 4: Explain how EBS auto-matches a customer payment.

Three-layer matching:

  • Interpretation algorithms identify transaction type from bank code/text.
  • Posting rules define which algorithms to run and in what order.
  • Search algorithms find matching open item by invoice number, amount, customer code.

If matched → auto-cleared. If not → goes to FEBAN for manual processing.

Scenario 5: ABC Group has 3 subsidiaries with mutual payables/receivables. How does IHC netting reduce transactions?

Without IHC: 3 gross cross-border payments.

With IHC netting: Subsidiaries post to IHC instead of each other. IHC computes net positions:

  • IN01: −50,000 (net pay)
  • SG01: +5,00,000 (net receive)
  • DE01: −4,50,000 (net pay)

Only 2 net payments made — 60–80% reduction. FX exposure also centralised.

Scenario 6: A raw material is standard ₹100/kg but purchased at ₹105/kg. 1,000 kg bought, 800 kg consumed. How does ML handle the price difference?

Purchase entry:

  • Dr Inventory — ₹1,00,000 (at standard)
  • Dr Price Difference (ML) — ₹5,000
  • Cr Vendor — ₹1,05,000

Month-end CKMLCP settlement distributes ₹5,000:

  • Inventory (200 kg remaining) — 20% → ₹1,000
  • COGS (800 kg consumed) — 80% → ₹4,000

ML account cleared. Inventory revalued to actual cost.

Scenario 7: Client wants to migrate ECC → S/4HANA. Top 3 FI/CO considerations?
  1. Custom reports referencing COSP/COSS/COEP/CE1xxxx — must be rewritten against ACDOCA. Budget significant time.
  2. New GL + Parallel ledgers — mandatory in S/4HANA. If on classic GL, full migration required.
  3. Material Ledger activation — mandatory in S/4HANA. If not currently used, full ML implementation + historical valuation migration needed. Often the longest workstream.

Additional: Business Partner migration (customer/vendor), FSCM Credit Management, classic CO-PA → account-based CO-PA.

Rapid-Fire Questions

FI vs CO?
FI = external/legal/statutory. CO = internal/management/cost objects.
Primary vs secondary cost element?
Primary = has FI GL counterpart. Secondary = CO-only (assessments, allocations).
Distribution vs assessment?
Distribution preserves original cost elements. Assessment consolidates under a secondary CE.
Cost center vs profit center?
Cost center = collects costs by department. Profit center = internal P&L and balance sheet.
House Bank vs Account ID?
House Bank = banking partner (HDFC). Account ID = specific account within that bank. Together they form BANKL + BNKACC.
EBS vs Lockbox?
EBS = processes the entire bank statement (all transactions). Lockbox = specific tool for automated customer receipt matching at high volume.
Cash Position vs Liquidity Forecast?
Cash Position = snapshot (today). Liquidity Forecast = projection (future).
What is IHC?
In-House Cash — group internal bank. Centralises cash, nets intercompany payments, reduces external transactions.
Material Ledger purpose?
Records actual cost of materials in multiple currencies and views. Enables actual costing and price difference settlement.
Standard price vs moving average price?
Standard (S) = fixed, differences tracked in ML. Moving Average (V) = recalculated with every movement, differences absorbed immediately.
Universal Journal?
ACDOCA — single table for FI + CO + AA + ML + CO-PA in S/4HANA. Eliminates FI-CO reconciliation.
What changed in ML for S/4HANA?
ML becomes mandatory. Integrated into ACDOCA. MLIT replaces older ML tables for most reporting.
