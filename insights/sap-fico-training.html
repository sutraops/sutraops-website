1 SAP & Enterprise Structure

Understanding the organizational units that define how data, documents, and reports are structured in SAP.

Meaning

What is it?

Enterprise structure defines the legal, organizational, and management boundaries within which business transactions are recorded. It answers: Which legal entity? Which facility? Which department? Which management unit? Every accounting document you post in SAP carries a piece of this structure — Company Code, Plant, Cost Center, Profit Center. Getting the structure right at the start prevents massive rework later.

Key Organizational Units

Client (Mandant)
Top-level container. Self-contained commercial unit with its own master data. Represents one production or test system.
Company Code (BUKRS)
Independent legal entity with a complete set of accounts. Balance sheet and P&L are produced at this level.
Controlling Area (KOKRS)
Management accounting boundary. Costs and revenues are analysed here. Multiple company codes can share one controlling area.
Business Area (GSBER)
Cross-company-code segment for internal reporting. Used for segment disclosures.
Functional Area (FKBER)
Classifies expenses by function — production, sales, admin, R&D. Required for cost-of-sales accounting.
Plant (WERKS)
Operating facility within a company code. Central to MM, PP, and product costing.
Cost Center (KOSTL)
Where overhead costs are incurred. Used to collect and allocate costs for internal management.
Profit Center (PRCTR)
Internal profitability unit. Produces an internal P&L and balance sheet by product line, region, or business.
Segment (SEGMENT)
Reporting segment for IFRS 8 / Ind AS 108. Typically mapped from profit center.
Storage Location (LGORT)
Sub-unit of a plant where materials are physically stored. Drives inventory postings.
Purchasing Organization (EKORG)
Negotiates procurement terms. Can be assigned to one or many company codes.
Sales Organization (VKORG)
Legal unit responsible for selling. Assigned to a company code for revenue postings.

Real-World Example

Scenario

Company: ABC Manufacturing Ltd., India.

Setup:

  • Client 100 — "ABC Production"
  • Company Code IN01 — ABC Manufacturing India Pvt Ltd
  • Controlling Area IN01 — shared by IN01 and its subsidiary SG01 (Singapore)
  • Plants: PL01 (Pune), PL02 (Chennai)
  • Cost Centers: CC-PROD, CC-QA, CC-MKT, CC-FIN
  • Profit Centers: PC-IND (Industrial), PC-CON (Consumer)

Common Errors & Fixes

"Company code not defined"
Posting a document without a valid company code.
Check OX02; ensure the company code is in the user's default or is entered on the document.
"Controlling area not assigned to company code"
FI posting with a cost object fails because the company code has no controlling area.
Maintain OX19 to assign the company code to a controlling area.
"Cost center does not exist on date"
Cost center has expired or is not yet valid.
Extend validity with KS02 or check the posting date against the master record.
"Profit center not defined for company code"
Profit center belongs to a different company code.
Use KE51 to assign the profit center to the correct company code.

Interview Preparation

Q1: Can one controlling area be assigned to multiple company codes?
Yes. A controlling area can be assigned to multiple company codes provided they share the same chart of accounts, fiscal year variant, and currency (or the controlling area currency differs but translation is configured). This is common in companies with multiple legal entities managed centrally.
Q2: What is the difference between a Business Area and a Profit Center?
A Business Area (GSBER) is a cross-company-code unit used primarily for external segment reporting under older standards. A Profit Center (PRCTR) is a controlling object used for internal management reporting — it produces an internal P&L and balance sheet and is often mapped to a Segment for external disclosures. Profit centers are more flexible and are the modern standard.
Q3: What happens if you change the chart of accounts after go-live?
Changing the chart of accounts after go-live is extremely disruptive — it requires re-mapping every GL account and re-posting historical balances. In practice, most companies add a new chart of accounts (parallel ledger) rather than modify the existing one. This is why getting the chart of accounts right at design time is critical.

2 General Ledger & Core FI

The heart of financial accounting — how every transaction is recorded, classified, and reported.

Meaning

What is it?

The General Ledger (GL) is the central repository of all financial transactions. Every posting in AP, AR, AA, or CO ultimately flows into the GL. A GL account is defined at two levels: the Chart of Accounts level (SKA1 — account number, description, account type) and the Company Code level (SKB1 — currency, open-item management, field status group).

Core Concepts

GL Account Master
SKA1 (chart of accounts) + SKB1 (company code). Determines whether the account is a P&L or balance sheet account.
Document Type
Two-character code (SA, KR, DR, etc.) that controls number range, posting keys, and reversal behaviour.
Posting Key
Two-digit code (40=debit GL, 50=credit GL, 31=credit vendor, 21=debit vendor) that determines the account type and field layout.
Number Range
Determines the document number sequence per document type and company code. Internal vs external ranges.
Field Status Group
Controls which fields are required, optional, or suppressed per GL account during entry.
Posting Period
Determines the open periods for a company code — normal periods (1–12) and special periods (13–16) for adjustments.
Parallel Ledgers
Leading ledger (usually 0L) and non-leading ledgers for IFRS, local GAAP, tax, etc. All postings flow to the leading ledger.
Parked Documents
Documents saved but not posted. Used for approval workflows. Do not affect balances.

Real-World Example — Posting a Journal Entry

Scenario

Interview Preparation

Q1: What is the difference between a posting key and a document type?
A document type classifies the entire business transaction (e.g., KR for vendor invoice, SA for GL document) and controls the number range, allowed account types, and reversal behaviour. A posting key applies to a single line item and tells SAP whether it is a debit or credit and what account type it is (GL, vendor, customer, asset). One document can have multiple posting keys but only one document type.
Q2: How does SAP ensure debits equal credits?
SAP enforces the double-entry rule at document save time. If total debits do not equal total credits, the system rejects the document with the error "Document is not balanced." This applies to every posting, regardless of module — FI, CO, AA all respect the same rule.
Q3: What is a field status group and why is it important?
A field status group (maintained via OBC4 and assigned in SKB1) controls which fields are required, optional, or suppressed when posting to a specific GL account. For example, a bank account may require the assignment field, while a P&L account may require a cost center. Getting field status groups right is critical to enforcing data quality at entry.
Q4: How do you reverse a document?
Use FB08 for a single document or F.80 for mass reversal. You can choose reversal reason (which determines whether the reversal posts in the same period or the next), reversal date, and reversal posting period. Negative posting (if enabled) reverses the original debit/credit signs without creating a new document.

3 Accounts Payable, Receivable & Asset Accounting

The three subledgers that sit beside the GL — each with its own master data, transactions, and reconciliation process.

Meaning

What is it?

AP, AR, and AA are subledgers — they maintain detailed records for specific account types and post summarised balances to the GL via reconciliation accounts. Each has its own master data, transaction codes, and reporting. At month end, the subledger balance must equal the GL reconciliation account balance — this is one of the most fundamental controls in any ERP.

Accounts Payable (AP)

Purpose: Manage what the company owes to suppliers — invoices, credit memos, payment runs, and aging.

Master Data: LFA1 (general), LFB1 (company code), LFM1 (purchasing).

Interview Preparation

Q1: What is a reconciliation account and why is it needed?
A reconciliation account is the GL account to which a subledger (AP, AR, AA) posts its summarised balance. You cannot post directly to a reconciliation account — every posting must go through the subledger. This ensures that the subledger balance always equals the GL reconciliation account balance at any point in time. Examples: 200100 (Vendor Reconciliation), 120100 (Customer Reconciliation), 150100 (Asset Reconciliation).
Q2: What is the GR/IR clearing account and how is it used?
GR/IR (Goods Receipt / Invoice Receipt) clearing is a suspense account. When goods are received (GR), the system debits stock/inventory and credits GR/IR. When the invoice arrives (IR), it debits GR/IR and credits the vendor. If GR and IR match, the GR/IR balance is zero. If not, the residual stays as a "goods received not invoiced" or "invoice received not delivered" balance, which is reclassified at month end using F.19.
Q3: How is asset depreciation calculated in SAP?
Depreciation is determined by: (1) Asset class (which determines the depreciation key), (2) Depreciation key (which defines the method, useful life, and rate), (3) Depreciation area (book, tax, cost accounting). The system calculates depreciation using the base value (usually APC — Acquisition and Production Costs) and posts it via AFAB. The entry is Dr Depreciation Expense / Cr Accumulated Depreciation.

4 Controlling (CO)

Cost and profitability management across cost centers, orders, and profit centers.

Meaning

What is it?

Controlling (CO) is the management accounting module. It captures costs and revenues by cost object — cost centers, internal orders, production orders, profit centers — and answers questions like: Where did the money go? Which department overspent? Which product is most profitable? Where can we cut costs?

CO is not a legal requirement like FI — it is a management tool. But it is where the deepest financial insight lives.

Core Concepts

Cost Element
The CO equivalent of an FI P&L account. Primary cost elements link to GL; secondary cost elements are CO-only.
Cost Center
Where overhead costs are collected. Managed by a responsible person. Used for departmental reporting.
Activity Type
Unit of output (machine hour, labour hour). Used for internal activity allocation between cost centers.
Statistical Key Figure
A non-monetary measure (headcount, square meters, calls) used as an allocation base.
Internal Order
A temporary cost collector for a specific purpose (marketing campaign, R&D project). Settled at period end.
Distribution
Allocates primary costs from sender to receiver using the original cost element. Preserves cost element detail.
Assessment
Allocates costs from sender to receiver under a secondary cost element. Consolidates many costs into one.
Settlement
Transfers costs collected on an order to a final receiver (cost center, GL account, asset, CO-PA).

Real-World Example — Cost Center Close

Scenario

ABC Ltd. runs a monthly close. The IT cost center CC-IT has collected ₹5,00,000 of costs. These need to be assessed to other cost centers based on headcount (statistical key figure).

  • CC-PROD: 200 employees (66.7%)
  • CC-MKT: 50 employees (16.7%)
  • CC-FIN: 50 employees (16.7%)

Interview Preparation

Q1: What is the difference between primary and secondary cost elements?
Primary cost elements have a corresponding FI GL account — they represent costs flowing from FI into CO (e.g., salary expense, rent). Secondary cost elements exist only in CO and represent internal cost flows (assessments, distributions, activity allocations). They have no FI counterpart.
Q2: When would you use distribution instead of assessment?
Use distribution when the receiver needs visibility into the original cost elements (e.g., when each cost element has a specific business meaning). Use assessment when the receiver only needs a consolidated view and the sender wants to preserve the anonymity of individual costs.
Q3: What is a settlement rule and why is it needed?
A settlement rule defines where the costs collected on an internal order, production order, or project are transferred at period end. It specifies the receiver (cost center, GL account, asset, CO-PA segment) and the distribution rule (percentage, equivalence number, or amount). Without a settlement rule, KO88 will not run.

5 Product Costing & Production Integration

How SAP calculates the cost of manufacturing a product and tracks actual costs on production orders.

Meaning

What is it?

Product Costing answers the question: "How much does it cost to make this product?" It uses the Bill of Materials (BOM) and Routing to estimate costs before production, and then tracks actual costs during and after production to calculate variances. It is the bridge between FI (accounting), CO (costing), MM (materials), and PP (production).

Core Concepts

BOM (Bill of Materials)
List of raw materials and components required to make one unit of the finished product.
Routing
Sequence of operations (work centers) required to produce the product, with standard times.
Cost Estimate
Calculated cost of the product using BOM, routing, activity prices, and overhead rates.
Costing Variant
Configuration that determines how the cost estimate is calculated (valuation, overhead, etc.).
Cost Component Split
Breakdown of the standard cost by component — material, labour, overhead, etc.
Standard Cost
The expected cost of the product, marked and released for the fiscal year. Used for inventory valuation.
Production Order
Cost object where actual manufacturing costs are collected (materials, labour, overhead).
WIP (Work in Process)
Value of partially completed production at period end. Calculated at month end and posted to FI.
Variance
Difference between actual costs and standard costs. Analysed by category (input, price, quantity, etc.).
Settlement
Transfer of production order variances to CO-PA, FI, or inventory at period end.

"Cost estimate not found"

"Standard cost not released"

"Settlement rule is missing"

Interview Preparation

Q1: What is the difference between standard cost and actual cost?
Standard cost is the pre-calculated expected cost of producing one unit, based on BOM, routing, and planned prices. It is used for inventory valuation and variance analysis. Actual cost is what was actually incurred during production (real material consumption, real labour hours, real overheads). The difference between them is the variance — favourable if actual < standard, unfavourable if actual > standard.
Q2: What is WIP and when is it calculated?
WIP (Work in Process) is the value of partially completed production at period end. It is calculated at month end using KKAO. For orders that are not fully delivered, the system compares the actual costs incurred to the portion of the order that has been delivered and posts the shortfall as WIP on the balance sheet. This prevents the P&L from being distorted by costs that relate to unfinished work.
Q3: Walk me through the settlement process for a production order.
At period end: (1) All goods movements and confirmations are complete. (2) KKAO calculates WIP for orders not fully delivered. (3) KKS1 calculates variances for fully delivered orders. (4) KO88 (single) or KO8G (collective) settles the variance to the receiver defined in the settlement rule — usually COGS in FI, or a profitability segment in CO-PA. After settlement, the order balance is zero.

S/4HANA — The Universal Journal (ACDOCA)

What changed?

In classic ECC, FI and CO stored data in separate tables: BKPF/BSEG for FI, COBK/COEP/COSP/COSS for CO. In S/4HANA, all of these are replaced by one table — ACDOCA, the Universal Journal. This single source of truth contains FI line items, CO line items, AA postings, ML postings, and CO-PA characteristics in one place. This eliminates reconciliation between FI and CO and massively simplifies reporting.

Migration Considerations

  • Data volume — BSEG + COEP + COSP + COSS + CE1xxxx into one ACDOCA table can be very large; archiving and aggregation strategy is critical.
  • Custom reports — any report written against COSP/COSS/COEP must be reviewed and often rewritten against ACDOCA.
  • New GL — S/4HANA requires New GL (classic GL is not supported). Parallel ledgers must be configured before migration.
  • Material Ledger — becomes mandatory in S/4HANA and is the basis for actual costing.
  • CO-PA — costing-based CO-PA continues to work but is stored in ACDOCA. Account-based CO-PA is the recommended model.
  • Credit management — the old FI-AR credit management is replaced by SAP Credit Management (FSCM).

Interview Preparation

Q1: What is the Universal Journal and why is it important?
The Universal Journal (ACDOCA) is a single, unified table in S/4HANA that stores all financial line items — FI, CO, AA, ML, and CO-PA. It eliminates the need for reconciliation between FI and CO because they are no longer separate ledgers. It simplifies reporting, reduces data redundancy, and enables real-time analytics.
Q2: How does account determination work in MM-FI integration?
Account determination uses the OBYC configuration. When a material movement occurs, SAP determines the GL accounts to debit and credit by looking at: (1) the transaction key (e.g., BSX for inventory, WRX for GR/IR, PRD for price difference), (2) the chart of accounts, (3) the valuation class of the material, and (4) the valuation grouping code (usually the plant). The combination produces a unique GL account for that posting.
Q3: What is the biggest challenge in an S/4HANA migration from an FI/CO perspective?
The biggest challenge is usually the volume of custom code and reports that reference ECC tables like COSP, COSS, COEP, or CE1xxxx. In S/4HANA these are compatibility views on ACDOCA, but performance can be poor if not optimised. Migration teams must review every custom report, rewrite it to use ACDOCA directly, and test it thoroughly. Data migration and reconciliation of the New GL also demand careful planning.

7 Period-End Close & Troubleshooting

The structured sequence of activities that closes the books each month, quarter, and year.

Meaning

What is it?

Period-end close is the process of finalising the books for a period. It involves cut-off activities, accruals, reconciliations, adjustments, FX valuation, and reporting. A well-run close follows a close calendar with defined tasks, owners, and deadlines. For most companies, the target is a "fast close" — complete within 3–5 business days.

Key Close Activities Explained

1. Accruals (FBS1): Expenses that have been incurred but not yet invoiced. Example: rent for the month, salaries for the last week, utilities. Entry: Dr Expense / Cr Accrual Liability. Reversed at start of next month.

2. Prepayments (F-02): Payments made in advance for future periods. Example: annual insurance premium paid in January covers 12 months. Entry: Dr Prepaid Expense / Cr Bank. Then monthly: Dr Expense / Cr Prepaid.

3. GR/IR Reclassification (F.19): At month end, GR/IR clearing account may have balances. F.19 reclassifies them to "Goods Received Not Invoiced" or "Invoice Received Not Delivered". Reversed at start of next month.

4. Foreign Currency Valuation (FAGL_FC_VAL): Open items in foreign currency are revalued at the closing rate. The difference is posted as an unrealised FX gain/loss. Entry: Dr/Cr FX Adjustment / Cr/Dr Unrealised Gain/Loss.

5. Depreciation Run (AFAB): Posts depreciation for all assets for the period. Entry: Dr Depreciation Expense / Cr Accumulated Depreciation.

6. CO Assessment (KSU5) / Distribution (KSV5): Allocates overhead costs from sender cost centers to receivers.

7. WIP (KKAO) / Variance (KKS1) / Settlement (KO8G): Closes production orders as described in Tab 5.

Common Close-Time Errors

"Posting period is closed"
A late invoice arrives after OB52 has closed the prior period.
Either reopen the period temporarily (with approval) or post in the current period with a note.
"Subledger does not reconcile to GL"
AP balance ≠ GL reconciliation account.
Run RFAGL_SUBLEDGER_RECON or FBL1N vs FAGLL03; investigate any manual postings to reconciliation accounts.
"Depreciation already posted for period"
AFAB has already run and cannot run again.
Use AFAB in restart mode, or reverse the prior run with AFABR first.
"FX valuation produces incorrect result"
Exchange rates have not been updated in the system.
Maintain rates in OB08 before running FAGL_FC_VAL.
"GR/IR account has a large unexplained balance"
Old GRs never invoiced or old invoices never received.
Run MR11 to clear small differences; investigate large ones with the purchasing team.
"Order cannot be settled — settlement rule missing"
KO8G fails for some orders.
Maintain settlement rules via KO02 in bulk or use KOB1 to identify problematic orders.

Interview Preparation

Q1: Walk me through a typical month-end close in SAP.
Day 1: Cut-off and GR/IR reclassification (F.19). Day 2: AP payment run (F110), accruals (FBS1), depreciation (AFAB). Day 3: FX valuation (FAGL_FC_VAL), CO assessment/distribution (KSU5/KSV5), WIP and variance (KKAO/KKS1). Day 4: Order settlement (KO8G), bank and subledger reconciliations. Day 5: Close prior period (OB52), publish reports. The exact sequence varies by company but the principle is to complete all adjustments before closing the period.
Q2: How do you handle a late invoice after the period is closed?
If materiality is low and the period is closed for internal reporting, post it in the current period and disclose in the notes. If materiality is high, reopen the prior period (with approval from the controller), post the invoice, and re-close. Reopening should be exceptional and documented — it signals a breakdown in cut-off controls.
Q3: What is the subledger-to-GL reconciliation and why is it critical?
It is the process of confirming that the AP subledger balance (sum of open vendor items in BSIK) equals the GL vendor reconciliation account balance. This is a fundamental control because it detects unauthorised manual postings to the reconciliation account. In SAP, use RFAGL_SUBLEDGER_RECON or compare FBL1N to FAGLL03. Any difference must be investigated and corrected before close.
Q4: How does foreign currency valuation work?
FAGL_FC_VAL revalues open items denominated in foreign currency using the closing exchange rate (maintained in OB08). For each open item, the system calculates the difference between the rate at posting and the closing rate, and posts an unrealised gain or loss. The entry is Dr/Cr the open item / Cr/Dr unrealised FX gain/loss. The revaluation is typically reversed at the start of the next period so that the actual settlement rate is used when the item clears.

8 Interview & Scenario Practice

Scenario-based questions, configuration reasoning, and end-to-end troubleshooting — structured for SAP FICO interviews.

How to Structure Your Answer

The 5-Point Framework

For any FICO scenario question, structure your answer as: (1) Business context — what is happening? (2) Configuration / process — what setup enables this? (3) Journal entry — what are the Dr/Cr postings? (4) Integration points — which modules are involved? (5) Common errors — what usually goes wrong and how to fix it.

Scenario-Based Questions

Scenario 1: A vendor invoice of ₹1,18,000 (including 18% GST) has been posted. Payment is made 30 days later with a 2% cash discount. Walk me through both entries.

Step 1 — Invoice (FB60):

  • Dr Expense / Inventory — ₹1,00,000
  • Dr GST Input Credit — ₹18,000
  • Cr Vendor — ₹1,18,000

Step 2 — Payment with discount (F-53):

  • Dr Vendor — ₹1,18,000
  • Cr Bank — ₹1,15,640
  • Cr Cash Discount Received — ₹2,000 (on base amount only)
  • Cr GST Adjustment — ₹360 (reversal of input credit on discount)

Key configuration: Payment terms (OBB8) with a 2% discount within 10 days. The discount is calculated on the base amount, not the GST portion, unless configured otherwise.

Scenario 2: A production order for 1,000 units has been fully delivered. The actual cost is ₹5,10,000 and the standard cost of goods received is ₹5,00,000. How is the variance calculated and settled?

Variance calculation (KKS1): The ₹10,000 difference is decomposed into:

  • Input price variance — material purchased at a different price
  • Input quantity variance — more material consumed than standard
  • Resource usage variance — more machine/labour hours than planned
  • Remaining variance — overhead differences

Settlement (KO88): Dr Variance (COGS) / Cr Production Order for ₹10,000. After settlement, the order balance is zero. If the settlement receiver is a profitability segment in CO-PA, the variance reduces margin by ₹10,000.

Scenario 3: Your client reports that the vendor reconciliation account in the GL does not match the AP subledger. How do you investigate?

Step 1 — Confirm the mismatch: Compare FBL1N (vendor line items) balance to FAGLL03 (GL line items) for the reconciliation account.

Step 2 — Look for manual postings: Run FAGLL03 on the reconciliation account and filter for manual document types (SA, DR). Direct postings to reconciliation accounts are usually the cause.

Step 3 — Check for missing postings: Verify that all subledger postings flow to the GL. Look for parked documents, blocked documents, or reversal errors.

Step 4 — Correct: Reverse the manual posting or use F-02 to post a correcting entry. Then document the control breakdown and recommend a new preventive control (e.g., field status group that blocks direct posting to reconciliation accounts).

Scenario 4: A user is trying to post a cost to a cost center, but SAP gives the error "Cost element not created for GL account." What do you do?

Diagnosis: The GL account being used in the posting is a P&L account that has not been linked to a cost element in CO. Without a cost element, CO cannot receive the cost.

Fix: Create a primary cost element using KA01 for the GL account. In S/4HANA, this is automatic — every P&L account has a corresponding cost element by default. In ECC, it must be created manually.

Prevention: Always create the cost element when creating a new P&L GL account. Add this to the master data checklist.

Scenario 5: Explain how a goods receipt in MM creates an FI document without any manual intervention.

Mechanism: When a goods receipt is posted (MIGO / MB31), SAP reads the material master to determine the valuation class, then reads the account determination configuration (OBYC) to find the GL accounts. The transaction key BSX (inventory) determines the debit account; WRX (GR/IR clearing) determines the credit account.

Entry: Dr Inventory / Cr GR/IR Clearing.

Integration: The document is created automatically in FI (BKPF + BSEG) with reference to the MM document. It also updates the Material Ledger in S/4HANA.

Common failure: If the valuation class or account determination is missing, the GR will fail with "Account determination for entry ... not possible." Fix by maintaining the valuation class in the material master and the account keys in OBYC.

Scenario 6: A client wants to migrate from ECC to S/4HANA. What are the top 3 FI/CO considerations you would flag?

1. Custom reports and enhancements: Any custom ABAP report that reads COSP, COSS, COEP, or CE1xxxx must be reviewed. In S/4HANA these are compatibility views on ACDOCA and may not perform well. Budget time to rewrite them against ACDOCA.

2. New GL and parallel ledgers: S/4HANA requires New GL. If the client currently uses classic GL, they must migrate to New GL and set up any required parallel ledgers (IFRS, local GAAP, tax) during the migration.

3. Material Ledger: Material Ledger becomes mandatory in S/4HANA. If the client is not using it in ECC, a full ML implementation and historical valuation migration is required — this is often the longest workstream in an S/4 project.

Rapid-Fire Questions

What is the difference between FI and CO?
FI is external financial accounting — legal entity view, statutory reporting, balance sheet, P&L. CO is internal management accounting — cost object view, departmental and product profitability, allocations, variance analysis. FI is mandatory; CO is a management tool.
What is a cost object?
Any object to which costs can be assigned — cost center, internal order, production order, WBS element, profit center. Cost objects are the backbone of CO.
What is a document splitting?
Document splitting is a New GL feature that ensures every FI document is balanced not only in total but also by dimensions like profit center, segment, or business area. It enables real-time segment reporting and full balance sheet by profit center.
What is the difference between a reconciliation account and a normal GL account?
A reconciliation account is a GL account linked to a subledger (AP, AR, AA). You cannot post directly to it — all postings must go through the subledger. A normal GL account can be posted directly. Reconciliation accounts ensure the subledger always matches the GL.
What are the different types of CO documents?
CO documents are created for cost postings, activity allocations, assessments, distributions, settlements, and internal order postings. They are stored in COBK (header) and COEP (line items). In S/4HANA they are integrated into ACDOCA.
What is a substitution and when would you use one?
A substitution is a rule that automatically fills or replaces field values during posting. Example: automatically derive the profit center from the cost center on every P&L posting. Implemented via OBBH. Used to enforce data completeness and reduce manual entry.
